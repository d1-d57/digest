Any function I can actually write down is measurable, right?
James E. Hanson. Expositiones Mathematicae (2025). Preprint: arXiv:2501.02693
(full text fetched, 157KB).

Abstract: In this expository paper aimed at a general mathematical audience, we discuss how to combine
certain classic theorems of set-theoretic inner model theory and effective descriptive set theory with
work on Hilbert's tenth problem and universal Diophantine equations to produce the following surprising
result: There is a specific polynomial p(x,y,z,n,k_1,...,k_70) of degree 7 with integer coefficients
such that it is independent of ZFC (and much stronger theories) whether the function
f(x) = inf_y sup_z inf_n sup_k p(x,y,z,n,k) is Lebesgue measurable. Also gives a similarly defined
g(x,y) whose measurability-for-every-r statement has the consistency strength of an inaccessible
cardinal, and further degree-7-polynomial constructions.

Introduction excerpt (Section 1, "Independence results and Gödel's L"): genuinely excellent expository
prose — motivates set-theoretic independence via a simple group-theory analogy (abelianity independent
of GRP via Z/6Z vs S_3), then builds the "great tree rooted at the empty set" mental picture for models
of ZFC, height (large cardinals) vs width (forcing) vs pruning (inner models like Gödel's L), before
introducing the surprising measurability-independence construction. Explicitly aimed at a general
mathematical audience; genuinely bridges logic/set theory, number theory (Hilbert's 10th problem), and
classical measure theory/analysis.
