Predicament
Jordan Ellenberg — Quomodocumque blog, 2025-08-05

The author explores the etymology of the word "predicament." Originally, the term derives from "that which is predicated," essentially meaning any condition that can be described or specified — whether positive, negative, or neutral. This is comparable to "situation," which refers to "that which is situated."

The author argues that the modern English usage of "predicament" as "a difficult problem" represents a linguistic fossilization. They suggest this meaning likely originated from a forgotten euphemistic phrase, similar to how contemporary English speakers use "We have a situation" to diplomatically reference a problem.

Comments:
- Jon Awbrey noted that "predicables and predicaments are terms of art in Aristotelian and Scholastic logic," explaining these as philosophical categories rather than everyday language concerns.
- AG observed that "predicament" carries connotations of inevitability, distinguishing it from the more contingency-laden term "situation."
