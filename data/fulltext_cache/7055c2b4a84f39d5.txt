Applied Category Theory and Green Mathematics

Here’s a great, simple article about applied category theory:

• Natalie Wolchover, 
Can the most abstract math make the world a better place?
, 
Quanta
.

Natalie interviewed me twice for this, and it also features Matteo Capucci, Brendan Fong, Bob Coecke, David Spivak, Amar Hadzihasanovic, Nathaniel Osgood and Tom Leinster.

I’m glad that it explains a bit about ‘green mathematics’ and my struggle to do mathematics that will help the world.   I coined that term exactly 15 years ago 
here on this blog.

Hello Dr Baez,

Category theory excels at formalising relationships, but physical systems impose constraints categories don’t usually encode:

(1) Resource limits: morphisms have a thermodynamic cost (Landauer); signal and noise share a finite budget, so composition isn’t free.

(2) Gradual transitions: the same substrate can behave Newtonian or MOND-like depending on signal-to-noise; categories tend toward discrete types, not continuous regime changes.

(3) Time and persistence: relations persist at a cost; categories are largely atemporal. The open question is whether category theory can absorb these constraints or needs a relational/thermodynamic layer beneath it.

Looking forward to your thoughts!
